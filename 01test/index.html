Digital Citizenship

What is Digital Citizenship?

Digital citizenship is the responsible, safe, and ethical use of technology, the internet, and digital devices. It means understanding how to behave appropriately online while respecting others and protecting yourself.

What is digital law?

Digital law refers to the legal framework that governs the use of digital technology and the internet. It includes laws related to cyberbullying, online privacy, intellectual property, and digital rights.

What is AUP?

An Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) is a set of rules that outlines the appropriate and acceptable use of technology and the internet within an organization or institution. It typically includes guidelines on behavior, privacy, and security.

What is Plagiarism?

Plagiarism is using someone else's words, ideas, images, or work without giving proper credit and presenting it as your own.

Plagiarism is considered dishonest and can lead to consequences in school, work, or legal situations.

What is academic dishonesty?

Academic dishonesty is any form of cheating or unfair behavior in school or academic work. It involves breaking rules to gain an unfair academic advantage.

Academic dishonesty is considered unethical and can result in penalties such as failing grades, suspension, or other disciplinary actions.

What are the rules for AI generated content?

The rules for AI-generated content depend on the school, workplace, or platform, but generally users are expected to be honest about using AI tools, follow plagiarism and citation rules, verify the accuracy of AI-generated information, and avoid using AI for cheating or dishonest purposes. Some instructors or organizations may require users to disclose or cite tools like ChatGPT when AI assists with assignments or projects. Users should also protect personal and confidential information when using AI systems and follow any specific policies set by their school or employer.
